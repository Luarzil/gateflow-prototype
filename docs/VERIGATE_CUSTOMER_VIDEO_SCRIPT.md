# Veri-Gate — Customer Video Script

**Audience:** a Verizon integration specialist and a Cavalry / Enterprise operations decision-maker, possibly in the same room.
**Length:** approximately 3 minutes 40 seconds of narration.
**Tone:** confident, plain, no jargon. Never mentions Patrick, change requests, version numbers, or what was "fixed."

---

## How this differs from the Patrick video

The Patrick video answers *"did you build what I asked for?"*
This one answers *"what is this, and why would we run our gate on it?"*

Two audiences, one script. The operations person needs to see the gate move quickly and nothing get out that shouldn't. The Verizon specialist needs to see it is a real Android application that works without a signal. The script serves both by showing the work first and the technology underneath it second.

## Narration

### 1 — Title
> Veri-Gate. Vehicle movement control for gated facilities.

### 2 — The problem
> Every vehicle that enters or leaves a facility is a question. Who was driving. Were they allowed. Which vehicle was it. When did it happen. Most gates answer those questions on paper, or not at all — and the answer is needed weeks later, when something has already gone wrong.

### 3 — The handheld
> Veri-Gate runs on a rugged Android handheld at the gate. The operator sees one screen: the scan. No menus to get lost in, no settings to change by accident. Everything else in the system is somewhere else, on someone else's screen.

### 4 — Scanning a driver
> A movement starts with the driver. The operator scans the employee badge, or types the number if a badge is damaged. Veri-Gate shows who they are and whether their licence and authorisation are current — before the vehicle is even involved.

### 5 — Scanning a vehicle
> Then the vehicle. Scan the barcode on the windscreen and the make, model, colour and plate come straight back, so the operator can confirm the vehicle in front of them is the vehicle on the screen.

### 6 — Recording the movement
> In or out. One tap. The movement is recorded with the driver, the vehicle, the gate, the device, the exact time, and whether the barcode was scanned or entered by hand. Then the scanner returns straight to the start, ready for the next vehicle. At a busy gate, seconds matter.

### 7 — A vehicle nobody expected
> Vehicles arrive that are not on any list. A transfer nobody logged, a new unit, a contractor's truck. Veri-Gate accepts it, records the arrival, and creates the vehicle automatically. The operator is not stopped and not asked to make a decision. A vehicle arriving is rarely the risk.

### 8 — Stopping the wrong exit
> Leaving is different. That same vehicle now tries to go out — and Veri-Gate refuses. Its record is incomplete, so it stays until a supervisor completes it. Look closely: the driver is fully authorised. It does not matter. Being allowed in never means being authorised out. That rule is enforced in the database itself, so it holds even if something goes wrong on the device.

### 9 — An authorised exception
> Real operations need exceptions. When a driver is not authorised, the gate does not simply stop — a Fleet Lead or above can approve a temporary authorisation on the spot. Anyone below that rank is refused by name and by role, and the refusal is recorded. Exceptions become evidence instead of favours.

### 10 — The supervisor console
> On a computer, the same system opens as a full console. Same application, same data, different job.

### 11 — Completing an unknown vehicle
> Every vehicle the gate created automatically appears here, with the missing details listed. The supervisor fills them in, and the vehicle is released for normal use. Nothing was blocked forever. It waited for a person to confirm what it was.

### 12 — Drivers and authorisation
> Supervisors manage the driver roster, licence expiry, and temporary authorisations — individually or in groups. Drivers are records, never logins. Only staff have accounts, and what each account can do is set per person.

### 13 — Devices and locations
> Every handheld is registered to a gate, or assigned to one at the start of a shift. A device that is not ready cannot scan, so a movement is never recorded against the wrong location.

### 14 — Every movement, searchable
> Everything is searchable. By driver, vehicle, plate, licence number, gate or date range. When someone asks what left on Tuesday afternoon, the answer takes seconds and it is the same answer every time.

### 15 — Built for the yard
> Veri-Gate installs as a standard Android application. It runs on the handheld itself, so it keeps working when the signal does not — movements are stored on the device and upload automatically when the connection returns, marked as delayed so nothing is silently lost. No cabling, no gate hardware, no site works.

### 16 — Close
> Veri-Gate. Every vehicle accounted for, every exception on the record, every question answered in seconds.

## Recording notes

- Read at a measured pace; the timings above assume roughly 150 words a minute.
- Pause briefly between scenes rather than running sentences together — the video cuts on those pauses.
- The two lines worth landing hardest: *"Being allowed in never means being authorised out"* in scene 8, and *"Exceptions become evidence instead of favours"* in scene 9.
- A quiet room and a phone held a hand's width away will beat a laptop microphone.
- One take per scene is easier to edit than one long take.
